# Quiet Accounts: the Hyros pitch, adapted

A 15-minute call guide, the no-call page, a yearly offer that stays inside FTC rules, and answers to five objections.

**About the sources.**
- The Becker method (find the problem, let the owner price it, charge about 20% of that, then offer an annual VIP plan) comes from the founder's transcript. I couldn't find that transcript independently. The earlier conversion brief says the YouTube source "could not be retrieved". Use the method inside the company and never quote Becker to owners.
- Everything else is cited.
- **[H]** primary source or documented product behaviour. **[M]** secondary or self-reported. **[L]** anecdote. **MODEL** means my own arithmetic, not evidence.
- I read the competitor reviews through a page summarizer. Re-check the exact wording on the source page before any of it goes public.

## 0. The whole thing on one screen

| Becker step | What it becomes at Quiet Accounts | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The prospect names the problem and puts a dollar value on it | The owner guesses how many quotes go quiet and what a lead costs. Then his own file shows the real count. | Owner's answers, then the Quote Audit on his own export |
| 2. Show the product fixing *their* problem, and they say what it's worth | The audit writes note 1 to his most valuable recent quiet quote. He says how many of his quiet quotes he'd take back. | His file, his typical quote, his own guess |
| 3. Sell at about 20% of the value | $497/mo ($5,964 a year) against **the lower of** his guess and the rate the named shops got. First 150 free. A quiet month is free. | His number |
| 4. Annual VIP with a refund if results don't come | $4,970 a year (12 for 10). If the jobs on his ledger add up to less than he paid, he gets the difference back. Offered only to shops the numbers clearly fit. No auto-renew. | His ledger and the gate |

**Five rules that change how the script reads:**
1. **Price against the owner's number only when his number is honest.** If his guess is above what the named shops got (4 of 150 old quotes), price against theirs. A fee that's 15% of an inflated number is how Hatch-style disappointment starts.
2. **Ask three questions, then let the file answer.** Becker says keep asking until they admit the problem. Here, the file does the admitting. If the file is clean, say so and end the call. That is also the trust move.
3. **Offer the yearly plan mostly at the close after the free 150,** when the owner has his own ledger. On a first call, offer it only when the careful forecast clears 5×.
4. **The VIP extras are people time and terms, not product features.** Monthly is "$497, everything". Taking features away from monthly to fill the yearly plan would make that line false.
5. **"No risk" never appears in words** (the lint blocks "risk-free"). Say what actually happens instead: "$0 for your first 150. $0 in any month nobody asks for a price or a date. The yearly plan can't cost you more than your ledger brought in."

### A3. The script, minute by minute

**0:00 · Frame**

> "Thanks for making the time. Here's how I'd use fifteen minutes. I'll ask you five or six questions, then we'll run your quotes through the audit on your screen. It takes about ten seconds. If there's nothing sitting in there, I'll tell you, and you'll have lost ten minutes. Fair?"

**1:00 · IDENTIFY: the exact questions, in this order**

1. "What made you take the call?" *He tells you what kind of prospect he is (see the table below).*
2. "In a normal month in season, about how many quotes do you send?"
3. "What's a typical job for you? Not the biggest, the usual one."
4. "Out of ten quotes, how many turn into work?"
5. "The ones that don't: how many actually told you no, and how many just stopped answering?"
6. "What happens to a quote nobody's answered after two weeks? Who touches it, and how many times?"
7. **Lead spend:** "What does it cost you to get one new request? Google, Angi, Thumbtack, the truck wrap, whatever you pay for." *If he doesn't know, give him the published average and let him correct it (the benchmark table is under A3).*
8. **The value question. He does the math out loud, you don't:**

> "So call it [N] a month that go quiet, at about [$T] each. That's [$N×T] a month that nobody said no to. If a few of those came back every month, what's that worth to you over a year?"

Let him answer. Then:

> "Write that down. That's your number, not mine."

**Making the problem feel big (Becker's 10–20%) with his own file, not a promise.** The audit prints "That's N cents of every quoted dollar, and nobody said no to it." When N lands between 10 and 20, stop and let it sit. The only outside figure you may add, with its attribution: "In ServiceTitan's 2025 survey, nearly half of contractors doing $10M+ said following up on estimates brings in 11–15% of their income." That's self-reported and mostly HVAC and plumbing shops **[M]** ([ServiceTitan/Thrive 2025](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/residential-industry-report-2025), as in `claims.ts`). For tree shops only: "ArboStar's tree data says about 39% of estimates never turn into a job" **[M]**, vendor data with the sample size not disclosed ([ArboStar](https://arbostar.com/education-hub/where-tree-service-companies-actually-lose-money)).

**Lead-cost fallbacks if he has no idea.** These are search-ad cost per lead from LocaliQ, "a sample of 3,211 US-based search advertising campaigns from home services businesses running between April 1, 2024, and March 21, 2025" **[M]** ([LocaliQ 2025](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-search-advertising-benchmarks/)).

| Trade | Figure to use | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaning | $46.99 | Cleaning/Maid |
| Painting | $138.38 | Paint & Painting |
| Tree, fence | $90.92 | Home-services average (LocaliQ has no tree or fence line). Landscaping is $117.92 |

Always say "about" and ask "higher or lower for you?"

**The three kinds of prospect**

| Type | What they say | What Jack does | Exact lines | When to stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Knows the problem** | "I'm terrible at follow-up." "Stuff falls through the cracks." | Don't sell him the problem he already owns. Get the dollar figure (Q7–Q8) and get to the screen fast. | "Most owners say that. Let's see what it's costing you." | — |
| **2. Sees the symptom, not the cause** | "Leads are junk." "Close rate's down." "Everyone's price shopping." "Dead in November." "Angi's too expensive." | Trace it back to what happens after the quote. | "Walk me through the last three that didn't book. Did they tell you no, or stop answering?" If they stopped answering: "So it's not the lead. It's what happens after the quote." Then Q8. | Once he says "they just never got back to me" |
| **3. Denies it** | "We follow up on everything." "Jobber does it." "If they want me, they'll call." | Don't argue. Ask **three checking questions at most**, then let the file settle it. | (a) "How many times does a quote get followed up, and by who?" (b) "What happens after Jobber's second reminder?" (c) "If I opened last April's quotes right now, how many would still say awaiting response?" Then: "Let's let the file settle it. If it's clean, I'll tell you." | After three questions. If the file is clean: "You're in the top few. You don't need me. Keep doing what you're doing." End the call. |

The honest exit matters as much as the sale. It's the "never bill a man who got nothing" rule from the Call Flow, applied before he's even a customer.

**4:00 · SHOW: the Quote Audit on screen**

Setup:

> "I'm texting you a link. Open it on your computer and drop the quotes file on it. It runs on your computer; the file doesn't come to me. Share your screen, or just read me the numbers."

No file? "Here's a sample [tree] company so you can see what it does. The real one needs your file." The page labels the sample.

Walk the screens in the order the audit renders them (`apps/site/src/main.ts`, `render()`):

1. **The big number: "$X is sitting in quotes nobody answered."** Say: "Is that about what you guessed, or more?" Then stop talking. The gap between his guess (Q5) and his file is the Hyros "the platform says X, what really happened is Y" moment.
2. **"Your software shows N quotes sent" next to "What actually happened": Became work / Said no / Never answered.** Say: "Look at the 'Said no' line. That's everyone who actually told you no. Everything in 'Never answered', nobody told you no."
3. **The per-month line: "Every month about $Y of what you quote goes quiet. That's N cents of every quoted dollar."** Say: "Is that a number you'd want back?"
4. **The age buckets.** Say: "The last six months are the warmest. Those go first."
5. **Lead spend.** Say: "You said a lead costs you about $L. [N] of these went quiet. That's roughly $[N×L] you paid to get people who never heard from you again." Then the founder's line: **"You spent the time getting a lead. We make sure it doesn't get wasted."** *The page doesn't take a lead cost yet; do this line by hand (see B3).*
6. **The call list.** Say: "These [n] are big enough that you should call them yourself. We don't email them. You get the list, biggest first."
7. **The first note, already written to his most valuable recent quiet quote.** Say: "This is what [Karen] would get, from your office, in your company's name. Read it. What would you change?" If he starts editing, he's picturing it running.
8. **He says what it's worth:**

> "If [Karen] wrote back 'can you do October?', would you take that job?" *(yes)*
> "Out of the [N] quiet ones, how many do you think would do that if every one got asked?"

If he has no idea, anchor with named, labelled proof:

> "Two old-quote shops we've worked with each got 4 back out of 150. Dow's Tree in New Hampshire booked over $10k. Nelson Fence in Connecticut booked about $19,800. That's their own count, not a promise. Do you think you're higher or lower than them?"

These results are owner-reported with no comparison group **[M]**.

Then total it with him. Keep the backlog and the ongoing part separate, and never say "percent lift":

> "So [quiet in file] ÷ [his rate] × [$T] from what's already sitting there, plus [quiet a month × 12] ÷ [his rate] × [$T] from new ones going quiet. Call it $[his number] over a year. Sound right?"

Write his number in row 8.

**9:00 · SELL: $497 against his number**

New requests get answered, every quote gets followed to a yes or a no, past customers get asked back when they're due, every reply gets read, you get a text when someone's ready, and there's a ledger of every job it brings back. Any month nobody asks you for a price or a date, you don't pay. Cancel by text."

Then price it against his number. Use **the lower of** his number and the named-shop rate (4 in 150 on old quotes, 17 in 150 on past customers):

|---|---|
| $30,000 or more (price is 20% or less) | "A year of this is $5,964, less if any month is quiet. You told me about $[Z] is there. For every hundred dollars you told me is sitting there, this costs about [$P]. And in a month nobody asks, it costs nothing." |
| $15,000–$30,000 | "On paper it's not a slam dunk, and I won't pretend it is. That's why the first 150 are free. You'll see your own number before you spend a dollar." |
| Under $15,000 | "Honestly, at your numbers I'd start with the free 150 and let the result decide. I'm not going to pitch you anything else." (No yearly plan.) |

Add when it's true: "At your typical job of $[T], one booking covers the month." The site already computes this line.

If his guess is above the named-shop rate: "You said 1 in 20. The two old-quote shops got 1 in 37. I'd decide on theirs, not yours."

**Beating the other options, in 30 seconds (Becker's "we're the best option"):**

### A4. What not to say on a call

These come from `lintMarketing` and the research briefs.
- Never say "risk-free", "money-back", "guaranteed 15–20%", "AI-powered", "campaign", "blast", "drip" or "nurture". Don't use "leads" on its own; say "a lead you paid for" or "new requests".
- Don't use the unsourced stats (80% need five follow-ups, 44% give up, 98% SMS opens, 7× if you answer in five minutes).
- **Don't promise "answered in minutes"** until the ledger measures it. The conversion brief found that new-request answers currently queue behind follow-ups. Say "answered from your office, 7am to 8pm, every day."
- **Drop the $397 call-only first month** ("it ends when the day ends", from the Call Flow). It's a deadline used as pressure. It contradicts the "one price everywhere" honesty rule and Becker's own "logical sale, no pressure." Charge $497 everywhere.
- Don't mention Jobber Connect or the Marketplace listing until the app is published (Jobber prep rule P1).
- Don't say "a person reads every reply" if software sorts the replies. Say it the way the site FAQ does: "Software sorts it, a person checks anything that isn't a plain yes or a stop."

| When | Offer the yearly plan with the refund floor only if |
|---|---|
| **First call** (industry assumptions only, no data of his own) | His careful forecast is ≥ **$24,850** (5× $4,970) **and** at least **6 careful jobs**. This will be rare. |
| **The close after his free 150** (the default moment) | His careful forecast from **his own first-150 booked rate**, applied to the rest of his list at ⅔ for older quotes (a Call Flow assumption, **UNPROVEN**), is ≥ **$17,892** (the engine's Tier B bar, `forecast.ts:372`) **and** at least **6 careful jobs** |

Jack's hand version of "careful" on a first call: quiet quotes ÷ 65 × typical job. That is the engine's low rate of 0.015 for unanswered quotes. The named-shop "likely" rate is ÷ 37. Better still, the audit should print this for Jack (see B3).

## C. The yearly VIP plan, designed to stay inside FTC rules

### C1. The law in six lines

- **16 CFR 239.3(a) [H]:** "A seller or manufacturer should use the terms 'Satisfaction Guarantee,' 'Money Back Guarantee,' 'Free Trial Offer,' or similar representations in advertising only if the seller or manufacturer, as the case may be, refunds the full purchase price of the advertised product at the purchaser's request." ([eCFR Part 239](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-239))
  - A refund of the difference is not a full refund on request, so **never call this plan "money-back"**.
- **239.3(b) [H]:** a similar representation "should disclose, with such clarity and prominence as will be noticed and understood by prospective purchasers, any material limitations or conditions."
- **239.5 [H]:** "A seller or manufacturer should advertise that a product is warranted or guaranteed only if the seller or manufacturer, as the case may be, promptly and fully performs its obligations under the warranty or guarantee."
  - So the settlement must be automatic and on time.
  - Note: 239.2 covers only consumer-product written warranties. 239.1 says "the other sections of the Guides apply to the advertising of any warranty or guarantee."
- **16 CFR 251.1 (free offers) [H]:** "all the terms, conditions and obligations upon which receipt and retention of the 'Free' item are contingent should be set forth clearly and conspicuously at the outset of the offer."
  - So "first 150 free" must sit next to "then $497 a month only if you say yes."
- **ROSCA [H/M]:** the Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated in July 2025. The FTC opened a new ANPRM on March 11, 2026. ROSCA still requires sellers to "clearly and conspicuously disclose all material terms before obtaining a consumer's billing information, secure the consumer's express informed consent to the subscription, and provide a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges" ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/)).
  - Treat it as covering B2B. On the vacated rule: "The FTC has expressly clarified that the Rule will apply to B2B transactions – not just B2C" ([Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/ftc-finalizes-click-to-cancel-rule-key-compliance-insights-for-subscription-businesses/)).
- **The Hyros terms to avoid copying [H]:** "all refund requests must be placed in writing within ninety (90) days"; "Any chargeback … will result in an automatic disqualification from any refund"; automatic renewal "unless you indicate your intent to cancel at least 30 days before"; and the right to "amend pricing … at the beginning of each new Term" ([hyros.com/terms](https://hyros.com/terms)).

### C2. Verdict on the founder's wording

> "Pay the year up front ($4,970). If the jobs we trace to our notes don't add up to at least what you paid, we refund the difference."

**Safe in principle, and possible to administer, with five fixes:**

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Jobs we trace" sounds like we decide what counts | "The jobs on your ledger," with the counting rule written down (C3) and every line visible as it lands |
| "What you paid" is ambiguous once quiet months are refunded or he leaves early | "What you paid for the year, after any quiet-month refunds" |
| No date, so it reads like something he has to claim | "30 days after your year ends. Automatic. Nothing to claim." (This meets 239.5.) |
| Easy to slide into calling it "money-back" | Name it **the Year Floor**. Never "money-back" or "satisfaction guarantee." |
| Silent on renewal | "Nothing renews without your yes." |

Why the floor is "what you paid" and not "the number we discussed" (Becker's version): the owner's number is his own estimate. Guaranteeing it would be a performance claim we can't back up. The FTC order against HomeAdvisor was partly over lead-to-job rates it couldn't substantiate (research brief §10). "The year covers what you paid, or you get the difference" is the honest version, and it's strong enough.

### C3. The counting rule (publish it as "How we count")

A job counts on your ledger when all three are true:
1. **They had gone quiet before our first note.** That means a quote with no yes or no for at least 21 days, or a past customer who hadn't booked again.
2. **They answered one of our notes during your paid year.** Their reply is in the thread, and you got it by text.
3. **You booked work with them within 180 days of that reply,** before we settle.

**Also counted:** the same quote marked approved or converted in your software within 30 days after one of our notes, even with no reply. This line comes off if you tell us it wasn't ours.

**Not counted:**
- new requests we answered (even though we answered them);
- anyone who booked before our first note;
- replies from your free 150;
- cancelled or unpaid work;
- "came back after our note" matches with no reply and no same-quote conversion.

**Value:**
- the invoice amount in your software;
- if it isn't invoiced yet, the BOOKED amount you texted us;
- for recurring work (for example cleaning every other week), what they paid you from that booking up to the settlement date, capped at 12 months.

**Disputes over a line:**
- A line **with their reply to our note** stays. That's the rule.
- A line counted **only because a quote converted** comes off when you say "not ours."
- A line whose job your records show was booked before our note comes off automatically.

This closes the obvious gap: with a refund floor, a "Not ours" button on every line would let an owner delete his way to a refund. The rule is also stricter against us than the product's normal attribution, because it leaves out new-request answers and free-150 replies. That strictness is the point. It's the trust line: "We don't even count the new requests we answered."

**Settlement data:** the ledger (Reply Desk logs plus BOOKED texts) and the year's jobs or invoices export, which is the same kind of email he forwarded to start. If we can't get the export, we settle on the ledger as it stands, and the terms say so.

### C4. Can we administer it? Yes, if these are in place

- **A single automatic calculation.** Settlement date = end of year + 30 days. Refund = max(0, net fees for the year − ledger total), capped at net fees. Text the result, issue the refund, done.
- **Stripe mechanics [H]:**
  - Partial refunds are supported, but "you can't refund a total greater than the original charge amount."
  - "Stripe's processing fees from the original transaction aren't returned."
  - "Refunds use your available Stripe balance," so keep a reserve.
  - "Refunds for ACH Direct Debit payments must be initiated within 180 days from the date of the original payment." ([Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds); [Stripe ACH](https://support.stripe.com/questions/refunds-for-ach-direct-debit-payments))
  - So **take the yearly payment by card**, or pay any ACH-paid settlement by bank transfer or check. Refunds to closed cards can fail, so a check within 10 business days is the fallback.
- **Reserve:** hold 25% of each yearly payment (about $1,243) untouched until settlement. That covers the modelled expected floor cost in every scenario below except the worst one (fence at one-third of forecast, about $1,166 expected).
- **Chargebacks:** don't copy Hyros's "chargeback voids your refund" clause. Write instead: "If you dispute the charge with your bank, we settle through the dispute instead, so nobody's paid twice." Stripe itself warns about double refunds.

### C5. The gate: why 3× alone isn't enough (MODEL, UNPROVEN)

I simulated the floor alone (quiet-month refunds are a separate cost). Booked jobs are Poisson around the careful forecast; job sizes are lognormal around the typical ticket (σ 0.7). The "accuracy" columns ask what happens if reality comes in at 100%, 50% or 33% of the careful forecast. The research brief found our industry assumptions were "about 2x too high," so the 50% column is the realistic stress case.

**The founder's gate: careful forecast ≥ 3× $4,970 = $14,910**

|---|---|---|---|
| $900 | 0.5% / $4 | 24% / $333 | 56% / $1,075 |
| Cleaning, about $1,200 per customer-year | 1.3% / $12 | 28% / $460 | 57% / $1,248 |
| Tree $2,650 | 8.0% / $146 | 39% / $992 | 60% / $1,785 |
| Fence $4,500 | 16.1% / $413 | 46% / $1,508 | 62% / $2,259 |

**Recommended gate: ≥ $17,892 (Tier B, about 3.6×) and ≥ 6 careful jobs**

| Typical job (bar it implies) | Accuracy 100% | 50% | 33% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $900 (3.6×) | 0.1% / $0 | 13% / $150 | 42% / $698 |
| Cleaning $1,200 (3.6×) | 0.3% / $2 | 17% / $246 | 45% / $862 |

**Read:** a flat 3× is fine for small tickets. It's too thin for big-ticket trades, where one missing $5k fence job decides the year. The six-job rule fixes that without a trade-by-trade table, and it reuses the Tier B bar already in `forecast.ts`. On a first call, where the forecast rests on industry assumptions, use 5× (A5). This also squares the conversion brief's "5×" with the founder's "3×": use 5× before we have his data, and 3.6× plus six jobs after his free 150.

### C8. FTC fixes to current copy (quick list)

- **Cancel.** The FAQ says "Text CANCEL… you reply CANCEL YES." If an owner texts CANCEL and never confirms, then gets charged, that's the complaint ROSCA's "simple mechanism" is aimed at. Make **CANCEL alone** stop billing, and text back what's still scheduled with "Reply UNDO within 24 hours if you didn't mean it."
- **"Free trial" label.** The HQ invoice line "Free trial (first 150 quotes)" uses a term 239.3 names. The 150 is truly free (no card), so it's defensible, but say "First 150, no charge" instead.
- **Free-offer disclosure.** Every "first 150 free" line needs "then $497 a month only if you say yes" in the same breath (251.1).
- **Hero guarantee.** Change "We get every one an answer. Or you don't pay." to the quiet-month wording (B2 item 5).
- **Testimonials.** Keep "owner-reported, first 150, no comparison group" next to every result. Say "every shop that's finished a first 150" **only if that's literally true**. If any shop ran and did worse, it has to be shown or the claim dropped (Endorsement Guides, research brief §10).
