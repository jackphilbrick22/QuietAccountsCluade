01 · The thesis

Quotes don't say no. They go quiet.

A contractor pays for a lead, drives out, prices the job and sends the quote. Then nobody says yes or no. The owner is on a roof, the homeowner is busy, and the quote dies without an answer. The same happens to requests that never got priced, "yes" answers that never got scheduled, and past customers nobody asked back.

Owner-reported counts of each shop's first 150, with no comparison group. Old quotes booked at about 2.7% (4 of 150); past customers at about 11%.

The product is the system, not a dashboard. It reads the shop's records, finds every quiet quote, request and customer, writes one short note per person in the company's name, reads every reply, texts the owner the ones who want the work, and keeps doing it every month on everything new, with a ledger that proves which jobs came back. The owner never logs in.

03 · Rules

What every note obeys.

  • Three notes at most per quote or job, then it's closed out either way.
  • Checked twice: at planning and again at send, against the latest records. Converted, rebooked, do-not-contact, unsubscribed, bounced or commercial (unless turned on) never get a note.
  • Owner veto first: "go-away" prices (3× the shop's own usual), realtor/HOA/insurance bids and bad-customer tags are held for a look.
  • The call list: quotes of $10,000 or more and people with only a phone number are never emailed. The owner gets them, biggest first.
  • Stale prices: after 180 days a note never repeats the old number; the owner's hand-off says to re-price.
  • Only facts from the record: their job, their street, the month. No invented crews, dates, discounts or free visits.
  • An easy out: "Just reply 'pass' and I'll close it out." Every "pass" becomes a loss reason for the owner.
  • Any reply stops the sequence. A price or a date goes to the owner by text as soon as it's read; a stop is honored everywhere that minute.
  • Booked out: the owner texts BUSY until Nov 15 and new work waits until about three weeks before they open.
  • Seasons: no oak pruning offered April–October (oak wilt), emerald ash borer treatment timed to May–June, septic on its real interval.

05 · Showing the owner the problem

Their own numbers, before they give us anything.

Owners treat a 30–60% loss rate as proof their prices are right. Most of those "losses" never got a yes or a no. So the first thing they see is their own export, read in their browser in about ten seconds (the site's Quote Audit; nothing is uploaded):

  1. What their software shows vs what happened: quotes sent → became work · said no · never answered, with dollars.
  2. The monthly leak: "Every month about $32,300 of what you quote goes quiet. That's 51 cents of every quoted dollar, and nobody said no to it." (sample tree company)
  3. Lead money already spent on quotes that went quiet, when they tell us what a lead costs.
  4. The call list: big quotes and phone-only people worth a call from them.
  5. One finished note to their hottest quiet quote, with their company name typed in live. Then: "Follow up on your first 150 like this. Free."
Hi Mark, This is Sarah with Ridgeline Tree Co. We priced the oak for you back in August. Did you still want us to take care of that? If not, just reply "pass" and I'll close it out. Sarah

A real first note from the engine (sample data). The footer adds the company's postal address, why they're getting it, and a one-word stop.

06 · The offer

No risk to start, a reason to stay every month.

Start$0

We work your first 150 customers free. No card, no call, no contract. The owner reads and OKs the first note before anything goes out. Every yes is texted to them the same day.

Then$497/mo

One plan, everything: new requests answered from the office 7am–8pm, every quote followed up (three notes at most), past customers asked back when due, every reply read, the call list, the ledger. Cancel with one text; UNDO within a day.

Quiet Month Guarantee$0

Any month nobody we followed up with asks for a price or a date, that month is free. Answers to someone's own new request don't count against it. Automatic, counted on the ledger by published rules, texted before every charge. Never called "money-back"; nothing to claim.

Yearly, only when it's an easy yes$4,970

Twelve months for the price of ten. Price locked, a quiet month still refunds a twelfth ($414.17), nothing renews without a yes (asked 30 days out), and leaving early never costs more than monthly would have: the unused part comes back. Offered only when the free round alone beat a year's price, or the careful forecast is 5× it.

Why they pay month after month. The backlog alone takes months to work at a safe 75 new people a week. Then Every Month After keeps running on everything new: requests answered from the office (from Jobber or forwarded emails), fresh quotes followed up, regulars and service clocks coming due. And the guarantee makes every month justify itself. The weekly text shows fees paid next to jobs traced.

The Hyros lesson, applied honestly. Keep the specific promise on the same inputs ("Same quotes. Same Jobber. Nothing goes quiet."), measurement on real jobs, and done-for-you setup. Drop what hurt Hyros: a guarantee with a claim window, auto-renewal and a chargeback clause. The price anchors against the owner's own number: "Your typical quote is $5,300. One yes a month covers it."

07 · Forecasts and claims

We say less than we could, and all of it is true.

Fit tiers (screened on their own export)

  • Tier A: real revenue from invoices or jobs, careful year one ≥ 8%, likely ≥ 15%, dead quotes ≥ 1.5× revenue, warm work ≥ 0.4×. May be shown "9–16% in year one, 84% of it the one-time backlog". Never a recurring percentage.
  • Tier B: careful year one ≥ 3× a year's fee and 150+ reachable people. Shown in dollars only; qualifies for the guarantee.
  • Everyone else: the free audit, no promise.

On sample data, tree, septic, lawn and pressure washing reach Tier A; fence and painting reach Tier B; the cleaning sample misses Tier B by $34.

Claims

  • Jobber: 2 reminders, ≤90 days, not retroactive (Help Center).
  • About 1 in 5 pros answer a new lead within the hour (Jobber 2026, n=1,050).
  • About 4 in 10 tree estimates never become a job (ArboStar, 2025 data). Tree pages only, and never as "went quiet".
  • Nearly half of $10M+ contractors say following up on estimates brings in 11–15% of their income (ServiceTitan 2025). Never "you'll gain 11–15%".
  • What a request costs: about $38 tree, $33 painting and cleaning on Google Local Services ads (99 Calls 2026); about $138 for painting from Google search (LocaliQ). Angi charges for every match, win or lose (10-K).
  • 71% of homeowners put off a project last year (Angi); 68% say they'd hire the same company again (Housecall Pro).
  • Our three shops' numbers, labelled owner-reported.
  • "80% of sales need 5 follow-ups", "44% give up", "98% SMS open", "7× if you respond in 5 minutes", "78% hire the first to respond", "PCA: painters close 22%", "62% of calls go unanswered", vendor growth claims, "risk-free", "money-back", "guaranteed 15–20%". The copy linter blocks them.
  • Any industry-wide "X% of quotes go quiet". No such figure exists; the quiet rate comes only from the owner's own file.

How we count a win. Traced: the homeowner replied, or the very quote we chased converted (180-day window). "Came back after our note" with no reply is shown separately and never counted. Value is what was invoiced. One credit per job. A "Not ours" button removes any line. A comparison group (a staggered 10% who start ~60 days late) measures real lift; a single shop's group is "early" or "fair", never "solid".

08 · Trades

Tree, fence, painting and cleaning first.

Tree

Quotes die three ways: no response, shopping, or "got someone sooner". The note offers a real open week, never a fake one. No oak pruning April–October. Winter is the slow season, so dormant-season work gets asked for in fall.

Fence

Stalls on permits, surveys, HOAs and neighbors. The note removes the blocker ("we can check HOA rules and pull the permit"). Spring installs fill months out, so pre-book asks run in winter. Stain every 2–3 years.

Painting

Three bids and "we'll talk it over". Most painters never follow up; the one who does is remembered. Exterior asks respect the season; interior runs year-round. Phased scope ("just the front and sides") answers price.

Cleaning

The leak is regulars who skip a month and never return, and one-time deep cleans that never become recurring. Win-back at 30–45 days; "keep your regular day?" No free-look promises in this trade.

Every other trade runs on the same engine through its playbook (septic, lawn, landscape, pressure washing, gutters, chimney, HVAC, pest, pool, concrete and more). The trade is read from the shop's own job titles; nobody has to pick it.

09 · Compliance, enforced in code

The rules the software won't let anyone break.

  • CAN-SPAM: honest headers and subjects (no fake "Re:" on a first note), the postal address and a why-line in every commercial note, one-click unsubscribe, opt-outs honored the minute they're read, everywhere.
  • Deliverability brakes: pause at 3% bounces, 0.1% complaints, or 1% "who is this?" replies. At most 3 notes per quote. Addresses checked before sending.
  • No texting homeowners. Owners only. Marketing texts need stored written consent, 10DLC registration and DNC checks, so they stay off.
  • AI: sorts replies and drafts answers; the only thing it sends on its own is a fixed acknowledgment ("Dave will give you a call this afternoon"). Price, dates, discounts and scope go to a person.
  • Collections: never messaged. Unpaid invoices appear only as "cash to collect".
  • Jobber app listing: read-only on clients and quotes by default, nothing written back unless switched on, 3 notes max, quotes over $10,000 left for the owner to call.

10 · Differentiation

Nobody else combines these eight.

  1. Done for you. The owner drops one file (or forwards one email), taps Start and replies OK to the first note.
  2. The backlog and the whole lifecycle, not just new quotes going forward.
  3. Timing tuned to each trade and season.
  4. One-to-one notes about their own job, in the company's name, three at most.
  5. Replies read and routed as they arrive; the owner gets a text with the name, number and what they said.
  6. Proof from invoices, with published counting rules and a comparison group.
  7. Month to month, cancel with one text (UNDO within a day), and an automatic Quiet Month Guarantee.
  8. Jobber-first, CSV for everything else. Keep Jobber; we work from its export.

11 · What's built

The software behind it.

Engine

Reads Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, QuickBooks or any CSV; finds the 12 kinds of breakage; plans, writes and lints every note; reads replies; keeps the ledger (new requests we answered never count as ours); runs billing, the guarantee, cancel and undo, and the yearly plan. 830 tests.

Server

API, SQLite store, worker, webhooks, owner texts (OK, BOOKED 2400, BUSY, SKIP a name, CANCEL and UNDO, RENEW), sign-up from the site with the owner's file, a request-forwarding address per client, Jobber OAuth sync, Instantly sending, Claude reply reader and drafter. 219 tests, end to end.

Site

Drop a quotes export and see your quiet rate and what's sitting quiet in ten seconds, in the browser, with the first note already written. Start sends the file. Tree, fence, painting and cleaning versions.

Operator console

Every client by part: the Money Map, the Notes, the Reply Desk, Ready Texts and Every Month After (requests answered, measured answer time, new quotes followed). New sign-ups arrive ready to plan.
